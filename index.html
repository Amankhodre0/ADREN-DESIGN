Branding / 4 min read

How much does a logo cost in India?

Prices range from ₹500 to ₹1 lakh+. Here is what actually changes the price.

Read the guide

Typical ranges in India today (they vary by city and experience):

  • Templates and logo makers: ₹500 – ₹3,000. Fast, but not unique and often not trademarkable.
  • Freelancers: ₹3,000 – ₹20,000. Quality varies widely, so check their portfolio.
  • Design studios: ₹15,000 – ₹75,000. Research, custom concepts, revisions and files for print and web.
  • Large agencies: ₹1 lakh and above. Full brand strategy and guidelines.

What affects the price: number of concepts, revision rounds, research depth, usage rights and whether you also get a colour palette, fonts and a brand guide.

Tip: always ask for the source files (AI, SVG, PDF) and a written note that you own the final logo.

Web design / 4 min read

How much does a business website cost in India?

From a simple 5-page site to a custom store, here is what to budget.

Read the guide
  • Simple business site (3–6 pages): ₹15,000 – ₹60,000.
  • Custom design with SEO and CMS: ₹60,000 – ₹1.5 lakh.
  • E-commerce store: ₹75,000 – ₹3 lakh+, depending on products and payment setup.

Do not forget the yearly costs: domain (about ₹700–₹1,500), hosting (₹2,000 – ₹15,000) and occasional updates.

Tip: a fast, clear 5-page site with a strong call to action usually beats a big slow one.

Branding / 3 min read

Logo or full brand identity: what do you need?

A logo is one piece. A brand identity is how you look and sound everywhere.

Read the guide

A logo is enough if you are just starting, testing an idea, or need a quick, professional mark.

Invest in a full identity if you sell across channels (social, packaging, website, print) and want everything to look consistent.

A brand identity typically includes a logo set, colour palette, typography, patterns or icons, and usage rules. It costs more upfront and saves money later, because every new design stays on-brand.

Tips / 3 min read

5 things to include when briefing a designer

A clear brief saves time, money and revision rounds.

Read the guide
  1. Who you are: what you sell and to whom.
  2. Your goal: more leads, a launch, a rebrand?
  3. Examples you like (and dislike): 3–5 links or images.
  4. Practical needs: where it will be used, sizes, deadlines.
  5. Budget range: so the designer can suggest the right scope.
